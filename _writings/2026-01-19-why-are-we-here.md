# Why Are We Here? Thoughts at the Beginning of 2026

The Nopili rock-climbing goby is a fish endemic to the islands of Hawaii. It climbs waterfalls against the current, with drops of water falling like bombs, using its fins and a thin film of water to maintain its grip. Many die in their attempt to reach the top. Against all odds, some make it, where they rest and breed. In time, their young are swept downstream, and the cycle continues.

One might wonder: what is the point of this Sisyphean existence?

But the larger question is whether human existence is fundamentally the same, albeit with more bells and whistles to keep ourselves distracted.

Let's approach this from two lenses: empiricism and rational inquiry.

## What the Science Says

Let's begin with what we know with a high degree of confidence.

The current best model for the formation of the universe is the Big Bang theory. About 13.8 billion years ago, the universe began in an extremely hot, dense state. It has been expanding ever since. Empirical evidence, such as cosmic microwave background radiation and the redshift of distant galaxies, strongly supports this theory.

Still, many questions remain unanswered. We do not know what caused the Big Bang, what—if anything—happened before it, or whether time itself existed prior to that moment. Our current laws of physics break down at the singularity, and we lack a unified theory capable of describing the universe's earliest instant. The ultimate origin remains one of the biggest unanswered questions in science.

That said, the evolution of the universe from a fraction of a second onward is well supported.

### The Origin of Life

How did life arise on Earth?

While the origin of consciousness remains a profound philosophical and scientific mystery, we can still examine what we know empirically.

Geochemical evidence from ancient rocks suggests that life existed at least 3.8 billion years ago, shortly after the Earth cooled and liquid water became stable. All known life shares a common ancestor: we use the same genetic code, the same chirality of amino acids, and the same ATP-based energy system.

The 1953 Miller-Urey experiment demonstrated that organic molecules, including amino acids, can form from inorganic precursors under conditions resembling those of early Earth. However, we still do not know the precise sequence of steps that transformed chemistry into the first self-replicating, membrane-bound organism.

In essence, we do not yet know what bridges the gap between interesting chemistry and life.

Science currently offers several leading hypotheses:

- **RNA World Hypothesis**: RNA may have served as both the first information carrier and the first catalyst, predating DNA and proteins.
- **Hydrothermal Vent Hypothesis**: Deep-sea alkaline vents may have provided the chemical gradients and mineral scaffolding necessary for early life.
- **Warm Little Pond Hypothesis**: Surface environments with wet-dry cycles may have concentrated and polymerized early molecules.

### The Origin of Consciousness

What about consciousness?

Science can explain many functions of the brain, such as perception, memory, and reactions to stimuli. But it has not yet explained why these functions are accompanied by subjective experience.

Consciousness correlates tightly with brain activity, but correlation does not explain why any physical process should give rise to the feeling of seeing red, tasting coffee, or feeling pain, rather than merely processing information objectively.

One way to frame this is that we understand much of the hardware, but not the software.

### Before Birth and After Death

Did we exist before we were born, and what happens after we die?

Every atom in your body was forged inside stars billions of years ago. In a physical sense, the matter that constitutes you has existed for nearly as long as the universe itself. You also existed as a latent possibility encoded in the DNA of your ancestors.

But your existence as a conscious subject is a different matter.

The unique combination of your memories, personality, and self-awareness depends on the complex neural structure of your brain. Before your brain developed, there was no known structure capable of producing your subjective experience.

After death, the pattern of your thoughts and experiences may vanish, but the matter and energy that composed you are recycled back into the ecosystem and, ultimately, the universe.

The Epicurean philosopher Lucretius offered what is known as the symmetry argument: the time before your birth and the time after your death are symmetrical. You were not troubled by the billions of years before you existed, so why fear the billions that follow?

## So, What Is the Point?

There is much we do not know about what came before birth or what follows death.

What we do know is that we are here, and that our existence is fleeting.

In the grand scheme of the universe, we are specks of dust that exist for only a cosmic microsecond. In a hundred years, most of us will be forgotten. Our families and friends will be gone, and strangers will inhabit the homes we worked so hard to build.

And yet our feelings, relationships, and experiences are profoundly real.

We have the opportunity to live rich and meaningful lives.

Spend time with the people who make you happy. Pursue the things that bring you joy. Work toward your ambitions, but do not lose sight of the larger picture. Create as many memories as you can. Improve as many lives as you can. Leave the world a little better than you found it.
